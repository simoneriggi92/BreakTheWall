# Research: the lab, a designed object for every source

Date: 2026-11-03. The owner's question is whether the guided reading can become a lab, where the source is taken apart as a recognisable object. The owner's judgement on the lab prototype was that it is "a good base from which to develop this mode". The question breaks into three parts:
- what of the lab prototype the app can already produce from a disassembly;
- what is still drawn by hand for each source;
- how the hand-drawn part could be produced for any source, and what a plan should measure first.

## Evidence

- The lab prototypes.
  - [prototypes/object-lab](../../../prototypes/object-lab/README.md) draws the public fixture `importer-signals` as a console with a cam programmer.
  - A private lab, under the ignored `.local/prototypes/`, applies the same engine to one of the owner's own generated disassemblies: an Italian document in two sources (645 and 310 lines), 7 top-level parts with sub-parts, 8 top-level and 22 inner joints. Only its behaviour is described here.
- [prototypes/object-approaches](../../../prototypes/object-approaches/README.md): three earlier approaches (modules in a body, the round's line, a scene from the metaphor), with the 10-second test.
- The app on `feature/guided-reading`, under plan [P-20261102-guided-reading](../plans/P-20261102-guided-reading/plan.md) v4. It is implementing, G8.3 is open, and G9–G17 are not committed.
  - `src/core/source-reading.ts`:
    - `sourceReadingRoute`, `readingRoles` and `readingSteps` give the route and what each step does (frame, add, deepen, relate, margin);
    - they are computed from citations only.
  - `src/renderer/disassembly/GuidedReading.tsx`: the stack of open levels (G17), with the upper level transparent and pressable, going back with ↑ or Escape, and the minimap (G11).
  - `src/renderer/disassembly/ObjectSheet.tsx`: the 2.5D board.
    - Each part is a shape, placed where `layoutLevel` puts its plate.
    - Each joint is a line drawn by its kind.
  - `src/renderer/disassembly/shapes.ts`:
    - 18 shapes;
    - `shapeOf` picks one from `kind` (for argument and story kinds) or from `icon`;
    - tested in `tests/shapes.test.ts`.
- `src/core/disassembly.ts`. The contract:
  - `PART_KINDS` (12 kinds);
  - `partIconSchema` (37 icons);
  - `JOINT_FAMILY`, with three families (mechanism, argument, story);
  - optional `metaphor` and `lens` per part;
  - no field that says how the whole looks.
- `src/core/disassembly-generation.ts` (`structureZod`, `structureInstructions`) and `src/mcp/assistant.ts`:
  - the structure is one request, and the assistant route submits it with `submit_structure`;
  - nothing in either asks for an object.
- [R-20261102-depth-in-advance](R-20261102-depth-in-advance.md): generated disassemblies have one level of depth at most, and usually none.
- The owner's statements in this session (2026-11-03):
  - the plates and slabs answer nothing to "but what is it?";
  - the results so far were "pessimi";
  - the lab, with its progressive building, its stacked levels and its compact card, is the base to develop.

## What the lab does, and where each piece comes from

```
disassembly ──▶ route, steps, what each step does ............ from data   (already in the app: source-reading.ts)
            ──▶ progressive building (a part appears where      from data   (the app has add/relate per step;
                it is first cited, a cable where its joint is)               the lab draws ghosts, then the part)
            ──▶ stacked levels, return to the reading point ... from data   (already in the app: G17)
            ──▶ opened level: modules from a library, columns   from data   (lab only: kind/icon → module, joints → columns)
                by joints, stubs where outer joints enter
            ──▶ compact part card (lead, marked points,         from data   (lab only; the app's PartCard pattern)
                relations in words, folds)
            ──▶ THE WHOLE: body, each part's component, place,  BY HAND     (per source, in both labs)
                size, cable anchors, "in the object" and
                "where the analogy ends" texts
```

The data side is already general. The private lab took the owner's document (two sources, 30 joints, headings inside code blocks) without code changed for it. Only the body was drawn new.

Two things in the lab come from data in ways the contract does not fix. The prototype depends on them working, but neither is checked today:
- **Display values** come from a run's variables, matched to a joint's label by shared words.
- **The writer** is the sub-part that writes an outer joint, found by citation overlap.

The hand-made side has the same shape in both labs:

| Hand-made item | Importer console | Owner's document | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| body | a console on a bench | a control unit on a table | the answer to "what is it?" |
| component per part | 7, chosen from a cam programmer, registers, a printer, a key switch, a relay, a lamp panel | 7, chosen from a back plate, a power module, a card rack, a core block, a belt, a port tower, a tester | what each part looks like it does |
| place and size (`FOOT`) | 7 rectangles | 7 rectangles | the silhouette, and what stands next to what |
| cable anchors (`ANCHOR`, lamp positions) | 8 | 8 | where a relation visibly leaves and arrives |
| look and limit texts (`OBJ`) | 7 pairs | 7 pairs, plus one generic pair for every sub-part | the honesty of the analogy |

Both labs drew their components from one vocabulary. It is close to the 18 shapes the app already has:
- a cycle;
- a register with a display;
- a tape;
- a switch;
- a comparator;
- a panel;
- a conveyor;
- a chamber;
- a foundation;
- a rack.

A second private iteration (2026-11-03) gave sub-parts a face without drawing it by hand. Each face comes from the structure of the part's own cited lines:
- a drawer per register;
- a dial per row of a gauge table;
- a row of pegs per group of methods, the peg left empty when the source marks the row with ⚠;
- a card per catalogue entry.

A part whose lines have no such structure stays a plain module, so nothing is invented to look better.

A third iteration the same day extended the rule to every sub-part of that document. The body comes from the part's icon, from a closed list (file box, warning board, panel of sockets, knobs or checks, turntable, screen). The face comes from the first structure the body accepts:
- section headings;
- table rows;
- list items;
- numbered steps in a code block;
- the code itself, drawn as its shape and not its text.

The result on that document:
- 25 of 28 sub-parts got a face, and 3 stayed plain because their lines are prose only.
- Names taken from headings and tables read well. Names taken from list items, the bold phrase or the first words, are the weakest.
- Rows of up to five parts, ordered by joints with each part next to the parts it is joined to, kept every inner joint between neighbours.

The rule is tried on one mechanism only.

**What separates the lab from `ObjectSheet` is the composition, not the shapes.** In the lab:
- the body has a name;
- parts take places that mean something: inputs on one side, outputs on the other, foundations at the back, monitors on an upright panel;
- sizes differ by importance.

In `ObjectSheet`, the places come from `layoutLevel`, a layout made for plates, and every shape has about the same size. The result reads as pieces on a tray.

## The open problem: who composes the whole

| Option | Who decides the body, components and places | Cost | ⚠ Breaks when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Rules in the app** | `family` → body, `shapeOf` → component, a placement grammar over the joints (sources left, sinks right, what is depended on at the back, monitors on a panel) | no generation; deterministic, testable | every source of a family gets the same body; placement by joints alone may not find a silhouette |
| **B. An object spec from the model, from closed lists** | the model picks a body, a component, a zone and a size per part, and writes the look and limit texts; the app validates and draws, falling back to A | one small extra request per disassembly, after the structure; it needs the structure only, not the source | the library is too poor and the compositions look anonymous; the texts invent an analogy the source does not support |
| **C. One generated image per source** | an image model | an image per level and per state | the parts cannot be pressed; the object is not the same across levels and steps; labels are unreliable |
| **D. Hand-drawn objects for prepared fixtures only** | the author | authoring time per fixture | generated disassemblies, the product's main case, get nothing |

C fails the lab's own requirements: pressable parts, the same object in both states, and progressive building. D is a demo, not a mode.

The real choice is A against B, and the two are not exclusive. B needs A anyway, as its fallback and as the validator's target: a spec is valid when A's engine can draw it without overlaps.

**A measure decides.** A is free to try. If A's compositions are recognisable on the cases below, B is not needed, and generation does not change. If they are not, B's contract is the smallest addition that closes the gap:

```text
object (optional, presentation only, checked by core):
  body:      one of a closed list per family (console, control unit, bench, frame, ...)
  name:      the plate on the body, ≤ 48 chars
  parts:     per top-level part id →
               component: one of the library (cycle, register, tape, switch, panel, rack, ...)
               zone:      one of a fixed grammar (back, left, centre, right, front, panel, floor)
               size:      s | m | l
               look:      what it is in the object, ≤ 130 chars
               limit:     where the analogy ends, ≤ 130 chars
```

Places, anchors and cable routes stay the app's work, computed from the zone, the size and the joints. The model never gives coordinates.

## Constraints from the product

- **One loop for every subject.** The lab must serve mechanism, argument and story with one library and one engine. Only the bodies, components and joint styles drawn differ. It is not a mechanism mode.
  - ⚠ The lab has been built only for mechanisms (the importer and the owner's document).
  - Mill's argument was drawn as a load-bearing frame only in `object-approaches` (approach A), under the old engine.
  - No story has been drawn at all; the public eval case `kessmouth` is the candidate.
- **Every cable is a joint, and only a joint is a cable.**
  - Alignment, containment and conceptual relation stay distinct.
  - Paragraph order creates no links.
  - The lab keeps all three rules. A spec or a grammar must not add a connection through adjacency: two parts placed side by side must not read as joined.
- **The source stays in view** (the dock), and every drawn element leads back to cited lines.
- **The contract is versioned** (`schemaVersion: 1`). An optional `object` field keeps old disassemblies valid. Storing the spec beside the disassembly, as presentation, would avoid touching the contract at all; this is a planning choice.
- **No new dependency.** Both labs are hand-written SVG with a cabinet projection.

## Assumptions

- The data-derived pieces (steps, building, stack, opened levels, the card) carry over from the prototype into `GuidedReading` without changes to `src/core`. Evidence: the private lab, and the fact that `readingRoles` and `readingSteps` already give the same add and relate events.
- The recognisability the owner saw comes mostly from the body, the components' silhouettes and their placement, not from details drawn for one source (the cam profiles, the printer's rows). Unverified: this is what measure 1 below tests.
- A small spec request costs little compared with generation: it takes the structure JSON (a few thousand characters) and returns about 7 entries. Unmeasured.

## Risks

- **Anonymous compositions.** Whether drawn by rules or by a spec, a generic library can bring back "pieces on a tray". This is the risk the owner named, and the reason to measure before planning the renderer.
- **An analogy that misleads.** A wrong component (a printer for something that only reads) teaches something false. The look and limit texts are where this is caught, and B has the model write them. They must be checked against the part's role, or kept to a fixed text per component.
- **Arguments and stories.** No lab exists for them yet. A plan built on two mechanisms could fail the product's own rule.
- **Parts named after sections.** A tester of the private lab (2026-11-03) pointed out that some top-level names describe areas of the document ("architecture and contracts"), not functions. An object makes that visible: a component needs a function to look like something. This is a structure-generation issue (`structureInstructions` asks for "what the part is"), not a drawing one.
- **The drawing exposes missing joints.** In the private lab, what a register holds is defined in a section that belongs to another top-level part, and the disassembly has no joint between the two. The lab shows it as a reference, not a cable, so that no relation is invented. Each such case is evidence for the structure request, not something the renderer should repair.
- **Depth.** The stacked levels need sub-parts, and generated disassemblies seldom have them (R-20261102-depth-in-advance). Without depth, the lab is one level deep.
- **Plan state.**
  - P-20261102-guided-reading v4 is still implementing, with G8.3 open.
  - Its G9–G17 work is uncommitted, and part of it (`ObjectSheet`, `shapes.ts`) is what this mode would replace.
  - A new plan cannot start implementing while v4 is active.

## Measure 1, first case: an argument composed by rules

[prototypes/lab-argument](../../../prototypes/lab-argument/README.md) draws the public fixture `mill-harm` with option A, as an experiment.

**How it is built.**
- The whole is composed by a grammar for arguments, written once, that reads only kinds and joint types:
  - supporters stand under, joined by load pins;
  - what they hold up is a beam;
  - what the beam supports is a deck;
  - a limit is a clamp on the end of what it limits;
  - an objection is a wedge;
  - a rejected part lies in a side tray.
- No position, shape or text is written for this source.
- The "in the object" and "where the analogy ends" texts come from a library, one pair per kind.

**Possibility.**
- All seven parts found a place by rule. The result reads as a load-bearing frame: my judgement, not yet anyone else's.
- Opened levels keep the grammar's shapes: three limits as clamps, three consequences as rooms.
- The faces rule produced nothing, as expected: the source is prose. In an argument the identity comes from the kind, not from the lines.
- Untested shapes:
  - several claims;
  - a premise that supports a premise;
  - an objection aimed at a premise;
  - a story.

**Usefulness.** The load test is the product's run: take a brick out, predict, then see. The object adds two things to the run's text:
- what rides on the claim (the deck and the clamp fall with the beam; the wedge and the tray stay);
- where the remaining weight goes (the beam turns on the support that is left).

**What the test exposed.**
- **The contract cannot tell linked supports from convergent ones.** A beam on two pins says "both are needed". That matches Mill's run, where removing either support makes the claim fall. But for supports that each suffice alone, the static drawing would teach the wrong thing before any test. The difference is only in the run's verdicts, and only for the bricks the run lists. A `supports` joint would need a grouping ("together with") for the drawing to be right in advance.
- **A physical fall overstates an interpretation.** The run says that without sovereignty the claim "falls into another thesis", and marks this as an inference. A beam can only fall. The chip "Interpretazione: il testo non lo dice" and the library's limit text carry the nuance. The drawing does not.
- **Library texts are safe but generic.** They never invent an analogy, and they never say anything about this source.

**Not measured.** Recognition by people who do not know the source (the 10-second test), and whether the load test helps someone understand the argument better than the text card alone. Both need people.

## Measure 1, second case: the app chooses the representation

After the first case, the owner proposed (2026-11-03) that the app decide what to draw, per source, instead of one fixed approach: an atlas for material like Mill, an object for technical documents, scientific material and manuals.

The 2026-11-01 report had already made two proposals, but implemented neither:
- separate the representation (object or nested atlas) from the reading behaviour;
- use the atlas when a metaphor would need invented joints.

[prototypes/lab-atlas](../../../prototypes/lab-atlas/README.md) adds both: a chooser and an atlas composed by rules.

**The chooser reads only the disassembly.** Its signals:
- the share of logical or narrative joints;
- the share of parts that take part in a flow (`sends-to`, `causes`);
- the part kinds;
- whether the cited lines hold tables or code.

Its rule:
- logical joints at half or more → atlas;
- a flow through three quarters of the parts or more → object;
- in between → hybrid.

The thresholds are a guess.

**Results on 14 disassemblies:**

| Disassemblies | Atlas | Object | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 public fixtures | 1 (Mill) | 2 (importer, release) | 0 |
| 11 generated by the owner, all technical documents | 0 | 4 | 7 |

- **Mill → atlas.** 5 of 5 joints are logical, and no part is in a flow.
- **The project README the report had analysed → hybrid.** Its core of four parts is in a flow, and two parts about the project rather than the mechanism only frame it. The report had proposed the same split by hand: an atlas of the document around a working object.
- **The private lab's document → hybrid.** Four parts form the core. Three frame it, and two of these are the parts a tester had flagged as "areas of the document". The third is arguably part of the mechanism. It lands in the frame only because generation joined it with `depends-on`.

**The atlas holds up on Mill.**
- Every region is placed by rule.
- Containment reads at a glance: the limits sit inside "Confini del principio", the three freedoms inside "La regione della libertà".
- The unjoined rejected part lies apart.
- In the load test, the atlas states the verdict without staging a collapse. This removes the overstatement the frame showed in the first case.

**What this exposes.**
- **The choice depends on the joint types that generation writes.** On this corpus generation writes only mechanism vocabulary, so the chooser can only say object or hybrid. A source argued in prose, but generated with mechanism joints, would still get an object. The chooser is only as good as the family that the structure request picks.
- **`depends-on` carries two meanings.** It can mean "works with" (part of the mechanism) or "is framed by" (a document area), and the hybrid split cannot tell them apart.
- **The hybrid view is not drawn yet.** It is the case most of the owner's documents fall into.
- **The thresholds are unmeasured.** Nobody has judged whether the chosen view is the better one for a reader.

## Measure 1, third case: the hybrid, and one engine for every view

The owner asked (2026-11-03) to make the app adapt to the type of source, starting from the hybrid view. The owner will judge usefulness. [prototypes/lab-adaptive](../../../prototypes/lab-adaptive/README.md) loads any disassembly and composes each of the three views by rules.

**Two grammars are new.**
- **The bench, for a mechanism.**
  - Modules stand in columns by the order of the flows: senders before receivers, what is depended on before what depends on it.
  - Sub-parts stand on each module's lid, as the components its opened level shows.
  - The joints are cables.
  - Every top-level object had been hand-made until now. The bench is the first that is not.
- **The hybrid.**
  - The chooser's core stands on the bench, and the other parts lie around it as regions.
  - What the core depends on stands behind it, and what depends on the core stands in front.
  - Arrows join the frame to the core, because nothing flows along those joints.

**Opening follows the part.** A module opens into components, with faces from its own lines. A region opens into sub-regions.

**Result.** All 14 disassemblies (3 public, 11 the owner's) compose in all three views, and every opened level opens, with no error.

| Disassembly | Core on the bench | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| the private lab's document | four parts | three regions: one behind (the part the core depends on), two in front (the parts that read or check it) |
| the project README | four parts | the two parts about the project, behind |

**What this exposes.**
- **Width.**
  - Hybrid and bench views run 940–1550 units wide, so at 1280×800 their text is small.
  - Columns grow with the length of the flow, and wide modules carry up to five sub-parts.
  - A layout that folds long flows, as the opened levels already do, is the next layout task.
- **The rule-made bench is less recognisable than the hand-made console.** It reads as "a bench of modules", not as a specific machine. That is the price of generality, and the gap the object spec (option B) would close.
- **Placing the frame by `depends-on` direction is a guess.** It reads well on both hybrid documents. It has no other evidence.

## Measure 1, fourth case: one representation per source, chosen by a metaphor test

The owner then set the requirement (2026-11-03). For each source the app chooses **one** whole representation: an object or an atlas, with no mixed composition. The object is chosen only when a coherent metaphor represents the main functions and the documented relations without inventing links, causes or dependencies. The choice is made from concrete correspondences, not from the subject or from shares of joint types. Context, limits and clarifications can be annotations. The hybrid stays a separate experiment.

[prototypes/lab-representation](../../../prototypes/lab-representation/README.md) implements the decision as a **metaphor test**. For each metaphor in the library (the bench, the frame) it checks three things:
- every part has a component that does what the part does;
- every joint has a connection of its type;
- no connection asserts more than the source.

A load pin, for example, asserts "remove me and what I hold falls". That counts as documented only when the source's own load test says "falls" for that brick, not as an inference. Limit parts and ⚠ points become annotations.

| Source | Result | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| `importer-signals` | object (bench) | 0 breaks: 7 parts, 8 joints. The frame would break in 15 places. |
| `mill-harm` | atlas | The frame breaks in 2 places, both inventions: a collapse the source gives as an interpretation, and a collapse the source never states. The bench would break in 9 places. |
| `atlas-release` | object (bench) | 0 breaks. |
| the owner's 11 generated disassemblies | 5 objects, 6 atlases | Each atlas comes from one or two parts whose icon has no bench component (messages, map, ruler). Some of those parts are rules, plans or estimates, where an atlas is right. Others are gaps in the library. |

**What this exposes.**
- **The test is only as good as the library and the icons.**
  - A part maps by its icon, and generation picks the icon.
  - A missing component sends a whole source to the atlas.
  - Whether that is right depends on the part, and the contract cannot say whether a part is context. Only the limit kind and ⚠ points carry that today.
- **The frame's checks rely on the source's load test.** Without a load run, no supports joint can be confirmed, so every argument becomes an atlas. That is the cautious default, and it means the frame is reachable only for arguments that come with a load test.
- **Width is unchanged.** The bench for the importer is about 1250 units wide.
- **The run must speak the representation's language** (the owner, after trying Mill). The atlas's drawing said "loses its support" while the card said "falls", and the question spoke of making the claim fall.
  - The prototype now suspends a support and asks which justifications stay explicit in the excerpt, answering from the `supports` joints.
  - The disassembly's own reading comes apart, without its opening verdict word.
  - A note keeps the result apart from the conclusion's truth.
- **The verdict claims only what the test compares.** It reads "Tra le metafore disponibili, nessuna rappresenta questa fonte senza forzature", not "no object holds".

## Recommendation

Planning is justified, after two measurements, in this order:

1. **Rules first, at no usage cost.** Build option A in the prototype: a family's body, `shapeOf`'s component, and a placement grammar over joints and kinds. Draw with it:
   - the three public fixtures (importer, atlas, mill);
   - the story case kessmouth;
   - the owner's document, locally.

   The owner then judges, beside the hand-made labs, whether each object is recognisable. This also builds the lab's first argument and story, which are needed either way.
2. **The object spec, only if A fails somewhere.** Ask for B's spec for the same cases, draw it with the same engine, and compare.
   - The requests go through Claude via MCP, on the owner's plan, or through the app's routes.
   - Before running them, give the owner an estimate of the requests and tokens, and wait for their OK.
3. **Then one new plan for the lab mode**, not a v5 of guided reading. It changes the renderer, perhaps the contract and generation, and the plan's identity. It would contain:
   - the engine (cabinet projection, library, grammar);
   - progressive building;
   - opened levels as modules;
   - the compact card;
   - the spec and its validation, only if measure 2 justifies them.

**Before that plan, close v4.**
- Record the owner's judgement as G8.3: the stack of plates is not enough, and the lab is the base.
- Decide what of G9–G17 to keep. The route, the steps, the stack, the minimap and the source band carry over; `ObjectSheet`'s board would be replaced.
- Commit it, so that the new plan starts from a clean, recorded state.

## Open questions

- Is the object spec contract data (an optional field) or presentation stored beside the disassembly? It matters for the MCP route and for disassemblies shared between devices.
- Who writes the look and limit texts: the model (per part, risk of invented analogy) or the library (per component, generic)?
- Does the lab replace the guided view, or sit beside it as the default with plates as the alternative? Plan v2 kept plates as an alternative view.
